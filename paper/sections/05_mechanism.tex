\section{What the looped block computes on text}
\label{sec:mechanism}

If the loop iterated towards an answer, the state after each loop would carry a better and better prediction, and
running more loops at inference would help. We probed trained models loop by loop \lbl{(exploratory)}: on trunk
checkpoints at $32N$, we read the state after each loop $k$ with the model's own output layers (the logit lens of
\citealp{nostalgebraist2020logit}, which here is the same as running the model with $k$ loops), with a learned linear
$w\times w$ map in front of the output layers (in the spirit of the tuned lens of \citealp{belrose2023tuned}, but trained
on the next token rather than distilled from the model's own output), and with a learned linear head; the maps are
trained on 10M unseen training tokens and evaluated on 2M tokens of the main validation set.
Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} shows the three training regimes.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig4_text_mechanism}
  \caption{What the looped block computes on text (main-set loss of trunk checkpoints at $32N$; values above the top of
  a panel are clipped). \textbf{(a)}~Fixed loop count: a 40M middle block trained with 8 loops (mean of three seeds),
  read after each loop $k$ by running the model with $k$ loops, by a learned map in front of the output layers, and by a
  learned linear head. The model works only at its training loop count. \textbf{(b)}~Truncated backpropagation (80M
  whole stack, $r = 8$, gradients through the last 4 loops): the state returns to the same point every four loops.
  \textbf{(c)}~A random loop count during training (20M middle block, $r$ drawn from 1--8 at every step) contracts to a
  fixed point: flat from about four loops on, no better than the dense model, with or without truncation.}
  \label{fig:mechanism}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{A fixed loop count gives a fixed-length program.} Trained with 8 loops, the model works only at 8
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}a). With a learned map, the state after loop 4 already reads out within 0.1--0.2 nats of the
final loss, and loops 5--7 add little; the last loop is a large non-linear move that rewrites the state into what the
output layers expect, and 37--47\% of the tokens settle their prediction only there. Running fewer or more loops breaks
the model. This held in all three seeds at 20M and 40M and at 80M (one seed).

\paragraph{Truncated backpropagation gives a cycle.} With gradients only through the last four loops, the whole stack
learns a periodic cycle (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}b): the state returns to almost the same point every four loops, so
the model works at 4, 8, 12 or 16 loops and gains nothing from more. The same minima at 4, 8 and 12 loops appear at 20M
and in both probed 40M seeds, one of which also dips at every second loop.

\paragraph{A random loop count gives a fixed point.} Drawing the loop count from 1--8 at every step, as Huginn does with
a different distribution \citep{geiping2025scaling}, gives a block that contracts to a fixed point: the loss is flat
from about four loops to 32 (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}c; one seed at 20M, with full and with truncated
backpropagation). The model is robust to the loop count, but at its nominal 8
loops it is 0.05 nats worse than fixed-count training and no better than the dense model; random-loop training erased
the advantage of looping at this size. Parcae's models, trained with a sampled loop count at 140M--770M, keep an
advantage (Section~\ref{sec:multiplier}), so this may be specific to small models or to our distribution; we tested one
seed at one size.

\paragraph{The learning rate shapes the iteration but never makes extra loops help.} Lower learning rates give flatter
curves over the inference loop count. In S1, on the middle block looped 4 times at 10M, 20M and 40M, the loss varies by
0.36--0.77 nats over 4--16 loops at 0.5 times the dense rate and by 3.4--4.6 nats at 1.41 times. At every rate, the
better of 8 and 16 loops was worse than the training count of 4. On text, more loops than trained never helped.
