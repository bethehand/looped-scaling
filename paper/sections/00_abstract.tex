\begin{abstract}
When does running a block of layers again work as test-time compute? A looped transformer adds depth without adding
parameters, and two benefits are claimed: it behaves like a larger model, and more loops at inference solve harder
problems without a written chain of thought. We test both, on web text in a pre-registered study (111 models of 10M--160M
parameters, plus extensions) and on a synthetic serial task with predictions frozen before its main runs. None of the four pre-registered hypotheses held as stated, and the pre-registered effective-parameter
exponent is not identified. In exploratory analyses, each parameter of a model
whose middle block is looped 4 times is worth at most about one dense parameter at 10M--20M with up to 20 tokens per parameter and about 1.3--1.5 at 40M--160M with 20--40, rising with data; at 40M and the grid's learning
rate, a dense model of the same size and nearly the same depth recovers about half of this advantage, but not the part
that grows with data. At equal training compute and a shared
learning rate the dense model wins every measured comparison, and extra loops at inference never help on text. On
chains of table lookups, the same architecture computes the chain step by step inside the loop. Within the training
range this buys depth, but a chain of thought trained on every intermediate value is perfect there at under a third of
the 8-loop model's inference compute. Beyond the range only the loop extends: a model trained with 8 loops on chains of at most 8
steps goes from 0.04 to 0.83 accuracy on 12-step chains with 16 loops, and for most training regimes its own confidence
tells it when to stop; the chain of thought, whose prompt grows with the chain, does not extend. At equal loss, a
middle block looped 4 times saves at most 19\% of weight memory but costs 2.2--2.5 times the KV cache and decoding
time. Code, logs and results are released.
\end{abstract}
