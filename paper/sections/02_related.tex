\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Claim one: what a loop is worth.}
Sharing parameters across layers trades compute for parameters \citep{dehghani2019universal,lan2019albert,bae2025relaxed}.
Transformers that re-use parameters ``perform slightly better when comparing models with equal parameter count, but
slightly worse when accounting for reuse and comparing per FLOP'' \citep[App.~D.2, Fig.~17]{kaplan2020scaling}.

The law we test comes from \citet{schwethelm2026how}: $\Neff = \Nonce + r^{\varphi}\Nrec$, where $\Nonce$ counts the
parameters run once and $\Nrec$ those in the loop. With $\varphi = 0$ the looped parameters count once, as in the
model's actual size; with $\varphi = 1$ they count $r$ times, as in the unrolled network. Schwethelm et al.\ estimate
$\varphi = 0.46$ on FineWeb-Edu, and we follow their choice of two unshared layers before the loop and two after it.
This layout of a prelude (two layers), a core (four layers) and a coda (two layers), and the input injection described
in Section~\ref{sec:models}, follow Huginn \citep{geiping2025scaling}. Schwethelm et al.\ also find that backpropagating
through only the last $\lceil r/2 \rceil$ loops, as \citet{prairie2026parcae} do, lowers $\varphi$ from 0.45 (their
baseline refitted on the same budgets) to 0.38. That comparison spends the saved FLOPs on more tokens. Our first
hypothesis (H1, Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}) tests truncation at equal tokens and at equal FLOPs separately.

Other studies measure the value of a loop in different settings, with mixed results. Parcae \citep{prairie2026parcae}
finds that looping lowers perplexity at matched parameters and tokens, and that at a fixed FLOP budget the amount of looping
should grow with the amount of data. Looped models without sparse layers scale worse than non-looped ones at equal FLOPs
\citep{lee2026sparse}. With repeated data, the best loop count grows with compute \citep{chen2026growth}, and a bounded
function of $r$ extrapolates better than $r^{\varphi}$ \citep{chen2026moe}. Looping the middle half of the layers beats looping all
of them by less than 0.01 nats in single-seed ablations \citep{wang2026smelt}. A dense model of the same depth beats the
looped one in a controlled appendix of Ouro \citep{zhu2025scaling}, and plain recursion is worse than a vanilla model in
validation loss at matched compute \citep{bae2025mixture}. A 1.4B looped model beats a dense model of its own size on all
12 of its benchmarks, but not a 3.9B dense model of matched inference compute \citep{marchenko2026closing}. In
data-limited pretraining, looped transformers beat standard ones on downstream evaluations at 10M and 100M words
\citep{gulbahar2026squeezing}. None of these studies varies model size and tokens per parameter separately with several
seeds, which is what our grid adds.

\paragraph{Deployment.} Without sharing, the KV cache and the decoding time grow with the loop count
\citep{wu2025parallel,monea2026shared}. Ouro shares the cache during decoding only, and full backpropagation through a
fixed depth prevents sharing the cache across loops in \citet{huang2026towards}. Models pretrained so that only the first loop writes a cache
lose no quality \citep{monea2026shared}.

\paragraph{Claim two: loops as test-time compute.}
Recurrent networks solve harder instances by running more iterations, and they can ``overthink''
\citep{schwarzschild2021can,bansal2022endtoend}. Looped transformers with an input-dependent number of loops generalise
to longer inputs on algorithmic tasks \citep{fan2025looped}. Recurrent layers with input injection,
on top of position embeddings made for the task, help arithmetic generalise further to longer numbers
\citep{mcleish2024arithmetic}. Recurrent-depth transformers extrapolate to deeper chains of composed facts with more
loops, while vanilla transformers of equal parameters or FLOPs do not \citep{kohli2026loop}. The number of input
positions a loop resolves per iteration scales with the training length divided by the training loop count
\citep{zhang2026recurrence}.
Shallow transformers can also learn shortcuts for associative serial computations \citep{liu2022shortcuts}.

What a loop converges to matters. Looped and equilibrium models settle into fixed points or orbits
\citep{geiping2025scaling,logan2026pertoken,bai2019deep}. Those whose fixed point does not depend on the starting state
use extra test-time computation better \citep{anil2022path}, and looping gains concentrate on reasoning tasks
\citep{saunshi2025reasoning}. We read the state after every loop the way the logit lens \citep{nostalgebraist2020logit}
and the tuned lens \citep{belrose2023tuned} read layers.

A chain of thought \citep{wei2022chain} adds serial computation to a fixed-depth transformer \citep{merrill2024expressive}.
We train it from scratch on the written values, as in scratchpad training \citep{nye2021show}, and compare it with loops
on the same problems, together with pause tokens \citep{goyal2024think}. Filler tokens help on some algorithmic tasks
\citep{pfau2024dot}. RELAY aligns loop iterations with chain-of-thought steps \citep{yu2025relay}, and continuous
thoughts feed the hidden state back as input \citep{hao2025training}. Transformers do not generalise robustly to inputs
longer than those seen in training \citep{zhou2024transformers,cho2024position}.

Adaptive computation time \citep{graves2016adaptive}, PonderNet \citep{banino2021pondernet} and Mixture-of-Recursions
\citep{bae2025mixture} learn when to halt during training, and \citet{fan2025looped} take the loop count at which the
model is most confident. We instead compare stopping rules that do not use the answer, applied after training and fixed in advance.

Our chain-task results replicate, with five seeds, that looped models solve longer chains with more loops
\citep{kohli2026loop,zhang2026recurrence}. We add probes of every intermediate value, the effect of the learning rate,
the stopping rules, and comparisons on the same problems with a written chain of thought and with pause tokens.
