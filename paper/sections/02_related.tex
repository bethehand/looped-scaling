\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Claim one: what a loop is worth.}
Sharing parameters across layers trades compute for parameters \citep{dehghani2019universal,lan2019albert,bae2025relaxed};
transformers that re-use parameters ``perform slightly better when comparing models with equal parameter count, but
slightly worse when accounting for reuse and comparing per FLOP'' \citep[App.~D.2, Fig.~17]{kaplan2020scaling}. The law we test, $\Neff = \Nonce + r^{\varphi}\Nrec$ ($\Nonce$: the parameters run once; $\Nrec$: those in the loop), and the
choice of two unshared layers before and after the loop come from \citet{schwethelm2026how}, who estimate $\varphi = 0.46$
on FineWeb-Edu; the prelude--core--coda layout itself, with its (2, 4, 2) split and an input injection that maps the
concatenated state and prelude output, is that of Huginn \citep{geiping2025scaling}. They also find that
backpropagating through only the last $\lceil r/2 \rceil$ loops, as \citet{prairie2026parcae} do, lowers $\varphi$ from
0.45 (their baseline refitted on the same budgets) to 0.38, in a comparison that spends the saved FLOPs on more tokens;
our first hypothesis (H1, Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}) separates the two accountings.
Parcae finds that looping lowers perplexity at matched parameters and tokens, and that at a fixed FLOP budget looping
should grow with data; looped models without sparse layers scale worse than non-looped ones at equal FLOPs \citep{lee2026sparse}; with repeated data
the best loop count grows with compute \citep{chen2026growth}; a bounded form extrapolates better than $r^{\varphi}$
\citep{chen2026moe}; looping the middle half of the layers beat looping all of them by less than 0.01 nats in
single-seed ablations \citep{wang2026smelt}; a dense model of the same depth beats the looped one in a controlled appendix of Ouro
\citep{zhu2025scaling}, plain recursion is worse than a vanilla model in validation loss at matched compute \citep{bae2025mixture}, and a 1.4B looped model
beats a dense model of its own size on all 12 of its benchmarks but not a 3.9B dense model of matched inference compute
\citep{marchenko2026closing}. Recursive transformers beat standard ones in data-limited pretraining, on downstream evaluations at 10M and 100M words
\citep{gulbahar2026squeezing}. None of these studies varies model size and tokens per parameter separately with
several seeds, which is what our grid adds.

\paragraph{Deployment.} Without sharing, the KV cache and decoding time grow with the loop count \citep{wu2025parallel,monea2026shared}; Ouro shares the cache
during decoding only, full backpropagation through a fixed depth prevents sharing in \citet{huang2026towards}, and
models pretrained so that only the first loop writes a cache lose no quality \citep{monea2026shared}.

\paragraph{Claim two: loops as test-time compute.}
Recurrent networks solve harder instances by running more iterations, and can ``overthink''
\citep{schwarzschild2021can,bansal2022endtoend}. Looped transformers generalise to longer inputs on algorithmic tasks
with an input-dependent number of loops \citep{fan2025looped}; recurrent layers with input injection, on top of position
embeddings made for the task, help arithmetic generalise further to longer numbers \citep{mcleish2024arithmetic};
recurrent-depth transformers extrapolate to deeper chains of composed facts with more loops, while vanilla transformers
of equal parameters or FLOPs do not \citep{kohli2026loop}; and the positions a loop resolves per iteration scale with the
training length divided by the training loop count \citep{zhang2026recurrence}. Shallow transformers can learn shortcuts
for associative serial computations \citep{liu2022shortcuts}. Looped and equilibrium models settle into fixed points or
orbits \citep{geiping2025scaling,logan2026pertoken,bai2019deep}, those whose fixed point does not depend on the starting
state use extra test-time computation better \citep{anil2022path}, and looping gains concentrate on reasoning tasks
\citep{saunshi2025reasoning}; we read the state after every loop as the logit lens \citep{nostalgebraist2020logit} and
the tuned lens \citep{belrose2023tuned} read layers. A chain of thought \citep{wei2022chain} adds serial computation to a
fixed-depth transformer \citep{merrill2024expressive}; we train it from scratch on the written values, in the manner of scratchpad training \citep{nye2021show}, and compare it and pause tokens \citep{goyal2024think}, filler tokens that help on
some algorithmic tasks \citep{pfau2024dot}, with loops on the same problems. RELAY aligns loop iterations with
chain-of-thought steps \citep{yu2025relay}, continuous thoughts feed the hidden state back as input
\citep{hao2025training}, and transformers that write tokens do not generalise robustly to longer inputs \citep{zhou2024transformers,cho2024position}. Adaptive computation time \citep{graves2016adaptive}, PonderNet
\citep{banino2021pondernet} and Mixture-of-Recursions \citep{bae2025mixture} learn when to halt during training, and
\citet{fan2025looped} take the loop count at which the model is most confident; we compare answer-free stopping rules
applied after training, fixed in advance. Our chain-task results replicate, with five seeds, that looped models solve
longer chains with more loops \citep{kohli2026loop,zhang2026recurrence}; we add probes of every intermediate value, the effect of the learning rate, the stopping
rules, and comparisons on the same problems with a written chain of thought and with pause tokens.
