\subsection{Models}
\label{sec:models}

All language models of the grid (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}a) are decoder-only transformers with eight unique layers of
width $w$: pre-norm RMSNorm, SwiGLU
feed-forward blocks (hidden size $\tfrac{8}{3}w$), rotary position embeddings, attention heads of dimension 64, no biases,
untied input and output embeddings, a 16k BPE vocabulary trained on the training data, and a context of 1024 tokens. A
\emph{dense} model runs its eight layers once. A \emph{middle-block} looped model runs two layers once (the prelude),
then the middle four layers $r$ times (the core; below also the looped block), then two layers once (the coda), so it
executes $2 + 4r + 2$ layers;
a \emph{whole-stack} looped model runs all eight layers $r$ times. Each loop starts from a linear map of the previous
state concatenated with the prelude output (the embedded input, for the whole stack, which has no prelude): the input
injection, $2w^2$ parameters, as in Huginn \citep{geiping2025scaling}; the first state is zero. Every residual branch is scaled by $(8/12)^{-1/2}$ (eight layers
against a 12-layer reference) and by a further $1/r$ inside the core, meant to keep the best learning rate independent of
$r$ \citep{wang2026residual}; Appendix~\ref{app:training} checks this. Backpropagation runs through all loops or, for
\emph{truncated} runs, through the last $r/2$ loops only. The five sizes, named by their
approximate non-embedding parameters (10M to 160M), have widths 320 to 1280 and $N = 15.4$M, 26.8M, 50.1M, 92.7M and
179.6M, where $N$ also counts the output head \citep{porian2024resolving}, which is large at small widths with a 16k
vocabulary (Table~\ref{tab:sizes} in Appendix~\ref{app:training}).

\subsection{Data, training and the grid}
\label{sec:grid}

Every run except the data-order runs of S7 reads a prefix of one fixed-order stream, files 000--012 of FineWeb-Edu
sample-10BT \citep{penedo2024fineweb}, 10.4B tokens with our tokeniser of which the longest run uses 9.9B, so no run sees
a document twice. The main validation set
(below, the main set) is 50M held-out FineWeb-Edu tokens from file 013; three further sets of 20M tokens come from SlimPajama
\citep{soboleva2023slimpajama}, FineMath \citep{allal2025smollm2} and GitHub code (Appendix~\ref{app:training}). We use AdamW
($\beta = (0.9, 0.95)$, weight decay 0.1, gradient clipping 1.0), a learning rate per width from a sweep, which the
looped models share (Appendix~\ref{app:training}), and a warm-up--stable--decay schedule with cooldown branches
\citep{hagele2024scaling}: one constant-rate trunk per run, from which cooldowns of 20\% of the budget, decaying linearly
to zero, branch off at several budgets. A budget is given in training tokens per parameter, written $10N$ for ten tokens
per parameter. Looped runs have budgets of $10N$, $20N$ and $40N$; dense runs $5N$ to $80N$ ($40N$ at 160M). Each looped
size has ten cells: middle block with $r = 2$, $r = 4$ and $r = 8$, each with full backpropagation and, for $r = 4$ and
$8$, truncated; and the same five for the whole stack. With one dense model per size, three seeds at 10M--40M and one at
80M, and a 160M dense reference, the pre-registered grid has 111 runs. Seeds change the initialisation, not the data
order.

Extensions, reported as exploratory, add a 160M middle block looped 4 times and a second 160M dense seed, continue
10M models to $160N$ and 20M models to $80N$ (Appendix~\ref{app:training} lists the cells and seeds), and train 20M models with
a random loop count, a regime the pre-registration declared out of scope; ten supplementary experiments, S1--S10, ran under plans frozen before their runs
(Table~\ref{tab:supp} in Appendix~\ref{app:training}). Everything ran on four RTX 4090 GPUs, about 19
days of wall-clock time in all.

\subsection{Measurement}
\label{sec:measurement}

Losses are in nats per token on the main validation set. Between initialisation seeds the loss of the 20M dense model
varies by 0.014--0.024 nats at $10N$--$40N$ (0.003--0.07 across the looped cells at 20M); a difference of 0.01 nats
between runs that share a seed is small but resolvable, one between unpaired runs is not.

\paragraph{The pre-registered law.} The effective size of a looped model is $\Neff = \Nonce + r^{\varphi}\Nrec$
\citep{schwethelm2026how}, where $\Nonce$ counts the parameters run once and $\Nrec$ those in the loop; unlike
\citet{schwethelm2026how}, who count non-embedding parameters only, we include the output head in $\Nonce$, as in $N$. A
dense ``ruler'' $L(N, D) = E + A N^{-\alpha} + B D^{-\beta}$ is fitted to the dense runs
\citep{hoffmann2022training,besiroglu2024chinchilla} with a Huber loss in log space and block-bootstrap intervals
(Appendix~\ref{app:fits}); with the ruler fixed, $\varphi$ is fitted per cell. A joint fit of the ruler
and all $\varphi$ is the pre-registered secondary analysis.

\paragraph{Without a fitted law.} Three quantities need no ruler. The \emph{gap} is the looped minus the dense loss at
equal parameters and tokens, same seed. The \emph{dense size of equal loss}, $\Nstar$, is the size of a dense model that
would reach the looped model's loss on the same number of training tokens: we read each measured dense size's loss at
that token count and interpolate $\log N$ linearly in the loss. This gives the multiplier $M = \Nstar/N$ ($M > 1$: the
looped model beats a dense model of its own size) and a local exponent $\varphi_{\mathrm{loc}}$ defined by
$\Nstar = \Nonce + r^{\varphi_{\mathrm{loc}}}\Nrec$. The \emph{equal-compute gap} converts a looped run's training FLOPs
(counted over executed layers, including attention and the output head) into the tokens a dense model of the same size
could have seen for them, and reads the dense loss there off the measured dense curve; where that point lies beyond the
measured curve, two extrapolations bound it (Section~\ref{sec:equalcompute}).

\subsection{Hypotheses and labels}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

The pre-registration, frozen on 2026-09-23 before any training run, states four hypotheses with decision rules
(Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}): \textbf{H1} truncated backpropagation lowers $\varphi$, under both an equal-token and an
equal-FLOP accounting; \textbf{H2} a looped middle block has a higher $\varphi$ than a looped whole stack; \textbf{H3}
$\varphi$ saturates in $r$, so that a geometric form $\Neff = \Nonce + \Nrec(1-\rho^r)/(1-\rho)$ extrapolates better
than $r^{\varphi}$ to a held-out size; and \textbf{H4} the best $r$ is small under a training-compute budget and larger
under a deployment-compute budget. Throughout, results are labelled \lbl{pre-registered} (hypothesis and rule frozen before training),
\lbl{pre-specified} (written in a plan frozen before the runs it describes: the synthetic design on 2026-10-09 and the
three supplementary plans on 2026-10-13, 2026-10-15 and 2026-10-27) or \lbl{exploratory} (chosen after seeing data).
Every departure from a frozen document is dated in the deviation log. The supplementary plans call their experiments
exploratory in the sense that they lie outside the pre-registration; we label their results pre-specified because their
quantities and readings were fixed before their runs.
