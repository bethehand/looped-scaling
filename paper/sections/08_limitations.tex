\section{Limitations, pre-registration and reproducibility}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Scale, data and evaluation.} Models of 10M--160M parameters with a 16k vocabulary, far below the sizes at
  which looped language models are deployed, trained on one source (FineWeb-Edu, no document seen twice) and evaluated
  by validation loss only, without downstream benchmarks. The trends with size may bend; there are five sizes, one seed at
  80M (the pre-registration's option of three seeds there for the cells that decide H1 and H2, if time allowed, was not
  used) and one looped cell at 160M, and single seeds for the random-loop language models and the KV-sharing test.
  Data-limited or repeated-data regimes, where loops may matter more \citep{chen2026growth,gulbahar2026squeezing}, are
  not covered. Seeds change only the initialisation, except in the data-order experiment.
  \item \textbf{The mechanism on text} was probed on trunk checkpoints at $32N$, before cooldown, at 20M--80M; cooled-down
  models and 160M were not probed, and S1's loop-count sweep is on trunk checkpoints as well. The 160M multiplier and
  the 160M deployment ratios rest on a dense size extrapolated beyond the largest dense model. $M$ counts the output
  head, which the loop does not multiply (34\% of $N$ at 10M), and the chain-of-thought compute comparison counts
  layer passes, not latency.
  \item \textbf{Depth on text.} The dense ruler has eight layers at every size; the deeper dense models of S10 cover two
  sizes with one shape each and three seeds (18 layers against the looped model's 20 executed layers, narrow at 20M), and their learning
  rate was checked with one seed, its best at 40M being the highest rate tried, with no rule in the plan to extend the
  range and no further rate run; at 80M--160M, where the multiplier is
  largest, the share due to depth is unmeasured. No dense control of equal executed depth at the looped model's width,
  which would have more parameters, was trained on text.
  \item \textbf{Equal compute.} 62 of the 320 equal-compute comparisons rest on an extrapolated dense loss, bracketed by
  two methods, and the measured comparisons are not independent of each other.
  \item \textbf{Learning rates.} The dense rates come from a sweep at one budget; the check of looped cells at 20M tried
  rates only up to the dense rate, which nine of its ten cells preferred; looped rates were re-tuned for one cell at
  10M--40M with one seed (two at 20M), where the shared rate understated the looped model by up to 0.024 nats at 10M and
  its best rate at $80N$ and $160N$ was the lowest tried; the other cells and 80M--160M use the dense rates. On the
  synthetic task, ties in the learning-rate criterion went to the lowest rate, several models were best at the lowest
  rate tried, and extrapolation is sensitive to the rate.
  \item \textbf{One synthetic task family}, chains of table lookups with 6.6M-parameter looped models; extrapolation length
  varies widely across seeds, and a pointer-chasing control was not learned and was dropped. Probes show where a value
  can be read out, not that the model uses it. The chain-of-thought comparison covers one task and one recipe; its prompt
  grows with the number of steps, so whether a written chain extends on a task whose input length does not grow was not
  tested; the chain-of-thought models were supervised on every intermediate value and got no help with length
  generalisation, and continuous thoughts \citep{hao2025training} were not tried.
  \item \textbf{Deployment} was measured on one GPU type, for models of at most 160M parameters, with one KV cache per
  executed layer; KV sharing was tested only as a change after training, at 20M.
  \item \textbf{Exploratory analyses.} The multiplier, the equal-compute bounds and the probes of the language models were
  chosen after seeing data. The stopping comparison (S8) was planned after the frozen rule's results were known, though
  frozen before it ran, and its selection criterion picked the confidence rule at 0.99 that the text highlights only for
  $r = 8$ and the random loop count, and only once votes are set aside. The readings of P3c and P4 and the set of
  loop counts behind the ``more loops'' columns of Table~\ref{tab:chain} were fixed after the results, and the S10 readings at each model's best learning rate were computed by a script written after the results,
  from a rule its plan fixed. The pre-registered verdicts stand on their own.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Pre-registration, deviations and reproducibility.} The pre-registration was frozen on 2026-09-23 before any
training run, and its SHA-256 was committed to the repository the same day; the synthetic design was frozen on
2026-10-09 and the three supplementary plans on 2026-10-13, 2026-10-15 and 2026-10-27, each before its runs. Every later
change is dated in an append-only deviation log with its reason and its effect on the conclusions, including the changes
made before any data, and the three reviews (2026-10-14, 2026-10-15 and, of the paper, 2026-10-27) and two further
correction passes (2026-10-17 and 2026-10-28) that found and corrected errors, overstatements and incomplete disclosures
(Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). Among them, a verdict had been judged
with a quantity the frozen synthetic design did not specify, and an evaluation it specified (the state-rule stop of the
loops = steps model) was computed but not reported until the second review. Code, configurations, the per-run
results and training logs of all 242 language-model runs and 135 synthetic-task runs, the fits, and the scripts that make
the fits and the figures are available in \coderepo; the remaining tables and numbers are computed from these results.
Fits and figures run on a laptop CPU; the model checkpoints (about 560 GB) are not included.
