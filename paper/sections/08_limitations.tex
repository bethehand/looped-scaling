\section{Limitations, pre-registration and reproducibility}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Scale, data and evaluation.} The models have 10M--160M parameters and a 16k vocabulary, far below the
  sizes at which looped language models are deployed. They are trained on one source (FineWeb-Edu, no document seen
  twice) and evaluated by validation loss only, without downstream benchmarks. The trends with size may bend. There are
  five sizes, one seed at 80M and one looped cell at 160M; the pre-registration's option of three seeds at 80M for the
  cells that decide H1 and H2, if time allowed, was not used. The random-loop language models and the KV-sharing test
  have single seeds. Data-limited or repeated-data regimes, where loops may matter more
  \citep{chen2026growth,gulbahar2026squeezing}, are not covered. Seeds change only the initialisation, except in the
  data-order experiment.
  \item \textbf{Probes and measures on text.} The mechanism on text was probed on trunk checkpoints at $32N$, before
  cooldown, at 20M--80M; cooled-down models and 160M were not probed, and S1's loop-count sweep also uses trunk
  checkpoints. The 160M multiplier and the 160M deployment ratios rest on a dense size extrapolated beyond the largest
  dense model. $M$ counts the output head, which lies outside the looped block (34\% of $N$ at 10M).
  \item \textbf{Depth on text.} The dense ruler has eight layers at every size. The deeper dense models of S10 cover two
  sizes, with one shape each and three seeds: 18 layers against the looped model's 20 executed layers, and narrow at
  20M. Their learning rate was checked with one seed. At 40M the best rate was the highest one tried, the plan had no
  rule to extend the range, and no further rate was run. At 80M--160M, where the multiplier is largest, the share due to
  depth is unmeasured. No dense control of equal executed depth at the looped model's width, which would have more
  parameters, was trained on text.
  \item \textbf{Equal compute.} 62 of the 320 equal-compute comparisons rest on an extrapolated dense loss, bounded by two
  extrapolations, and the measured comparisons are not independent of each other.
  \item \textbf{Learning rates.} The dense rates come from a sweep at one budget. The learning-rate check of the ten looped cells at 20M
  (Appendix~\ref{app:training}) tried rates only up to the dense rate, which nine of the ten preferred. In S1, the rate of
  one cell, the 4-loop model, was re-tuned at 10M--40M, with one seed (two at 20M). There the shared rate understated the
  looped model by up to 0.024 nats at 10M, and at 10M, at $80N$ and $160N$, the looped model's best rate was the lowest tried. The other cells and 80M--160M use the dense rates. On the
  synthetic task, ties in the learning-rate criterion went to the lowest rate, several models were best at the lowest
  rate tried, and how far a model extends is sensitive to the rate.
  \item \textbf{One synthetic task family}: chains of table lookups, with 6.6M-parameter looped models. How far a model extends varies widely across seeds, and a planned pointer-chasing control was not learned and was
  dropped. Probes
  show where a value can be read out, not that the model uses it. The chain-of-thought comparison covers one task and one recipe, and its compute comparison counts layer passes, not
  latency. Its prompt grows with the number of steps, so whether a written chain extends on a task whose input length
  does not grow was not tested. The chain-of-thought models were supervised on every intermediate value and got no help
  with length generalisation, and continuous thoughts \citep{hao2025training} were not tried.
  \item \textbf{Deployment} was measured on one GPU type, for models of at most 160M parameters, with one KV cache per
  executed layer. KV sharing was tested only as a change after training, at 20M.
  \item \textbf{Exploratory analyses.} The multiplier, the equal-compute bounds and the probes of the language models
  were chosen after seeing data. The stopping comparison (S8) was planned after the frozen rule's results were known,
  though frozen before it ran. Its selection criterion picked the confidence rule at 0.99, which the text highlights,
  only for $r = 8$ and the random loop count, and only once votes are set aside. How P3(c) and P4 are judged, and the set of loop counts behind the \emph{More loops}
  columns of Table~\ref{tab:chain}, were decided after the results. The S10 values at each model's best learning rate were computed by a script written after the results, from a rule its plan
  fixed. The verdicts on the four pre-registered hypotheses do not depend on any of these
  exploratory analyses.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Pre-registration, deviations and reproducibility.} The pre-registration was frozen on 2026-09-23, before any
training run, and its SHA-256 was committed to the repository the same day. The synthetic design was frozen on
2026-10-09 and the three supplementary plans on 2026-10-13, 2026-10-15 and 2026-10-27, each before its runs. Every later
change is dated in an append-only deviation log, with its reason and its effect on the conclusions
(Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). The log includes the changes made before any data were collected, and the errors, overstatements and
incomplete disclosures found and corrected by four reviews (2026-10-14 and 2026-10-15 of the results, 2026-10-27 and
2026-10-28 of the paper), a reader's review (2026-10-28) and three further correction passes (2026-10-17, 2026-10-28
and 2026-10-29). The corrected errors include a verdict judged with a quantity the frozen
synthetic design did not specify, and an evaluation the design specified (the state-rule stop of the loops = steps
model) that was computed but not reported until the second review. Code, configurations, the per-run results and training logs of all 242 language-model runs and of the 135
synthetic-task runs of the learning-rate sweeps, the formal runs and the supplementary experiments, the fits, and the scripts that make the
fits and the figures are available in \coderepo; the remaining tables and numbers are computed from these results. Fits
and figures run on a laptop CPU; the model checkpoints (about 560 GB) are not included.
